At a glance

Three paths. Same crew, same install.

Whichever you pick, the design, the permits and the install are handled by the same team. The only thing that changes is how the money works.

Purchase · You own it

Pay once. Keep every kilowatt-hour.

Highest upfront cost, biggest lifetime savings. After payback, the power is essentially free and the system adds value to your home.

  • Best long-term return
  • Nothing owed to anyone
  • Any incentives go to you

Finance · You own it

Own it with $0 down and a fixed payment.

A solar loan swaps your utility bill for a predictable payment, often smaller from day one. The system is yours when the loan is paid off.

  • No cash needed upfront
  • Ownership benefits, spread out
  • No hidden fees

PPA · A provider owns it

No upfront cost. Pay only for the power.

A Power Purchase Agreement puts panels on your roof at no cost. You buy the power they make at a set rate, typically below the utility's.

  • $0 upfront, no loan
  • Maintenance typically included
  • Savings from month one

    Which option saves the most money?

    Over the life of the system, buying outright almost always saves the most, because there's no interest and no one else's margin. Financing comes close. A PPA saves less, but asks nothing upfront. The calculator above shows the gap for your bill.

    What is a Power Purchase Agreement, exactly?

    A third party owns the panels on your roof. You agree to buy the electricity they produce at a set rate per kilowatt-hour, usually lower than the utility's, with a small yearly increase written into the contract. You don't pay for the equipment.

    What happens if I sell my house?

    If you own the system, it goes with the house and can add to its value. With a loan, you pay it off at closing or transfer it where the lender allows. With a PPA, the agreement can usually transfer to the buyer, or you can buy the system out. We'll show you the terms before you sign anything.

    Do tax credits change the math?

    They can, and the rules have changed recently. We leave them out of the calculator on purpose so the numbers don't overpromise. At your site check we'll walk through what applies to you today, for purchase, finance and PPA.

    How does California's net billing affect savings?

    Under the current rules, power you use yourself is worth more than power you send back to the grid. That's why we size around a year of your actual usage, and why a home battery can raise how much of your solar you get to use. Our model assumes each solar kWh saves 80% of retail.

    Is the quote really free?

    Yes. The site check and the three-way proposal are free, with no obligation. If the numbers don't work for your roof, we'll tell you.
